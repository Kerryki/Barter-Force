Buying your first home is exciting and, for most people, a little overwhelming. There are new terms, new costs and several professionals involved. This checklist walks through the main steps in the order they usually happen in Québec.

## 1. Know your real budget

Start with what you can comfortably afford each month, not just the maximum a lender might approve. Include more than the mortgage payment:

- Property taxes (municipal and school).
- Home insurance.
- Heating, electricity and water.
- Condominium fees, if you are buying a condo.
- Maintenance. A common rule of thumb is to set aside a percentage of the home's value each year for repairs.

Lenders also check that you could still manage if rates were higher than today's. That is a safety margin built into the system, and it is wise to build your own as well.

## 2. Plan your down payment

The minimum down payment depends on the purchase price. In Canada, when the down payment is under 20% of the price, the mortgage must be insured against default. This insurance protects the lender, not you, and its cost is usually added to the mortgage. A larger down payment can reduce or remove that cost.

Ask about accounts that can help you save for a first home, such as the First Home Savings Account (FHSA), and the Home Buyers' Plan that allows withdrawals from an RRSP under conditions. Rules and limits change, so check the current federal information before you rely on a figure.

## 3. Get a pre-approval

A pre-approval tells you how much a lender is willing to lend and at what rate, and it can hold that rate for a period. It is not a final commitment. It helps you set a realistic price range and shows sellers you are serious. Ask whether the lender will check your credit and how that will appear on your file.

## 4. Remember the costs beyond the price

First-time buyers are often surprised by these:

- **Welcome tax.** In Québec, the transfer duty on property, commonly called the welcome tax (taxe de bienvenue), is paid by the buyer, and it is calculated on the price or value using brackets set by each municipality. Ask about the amount early, because it is due soon after the purchase.
- **Notary fees.** In Québec, a notary prepares and registers the deed of sale.
- **Inspection.** A professional inspection can reveal costly problems before you commit.
- **Moving costs and immediate repairs.**
- **Adjustments.** Some taxes and fees are shared with the seller on the date of the sale.

Ask for a written estimate of these amounts so there are no surprises at signing.

## 5. Make an offer with conditions

When you find a home, the offer to purchase can include conditions, commonly financing approval and a satisfactory inspection. Conditions protect you. Make sure you understand each one and its deadline, and take the time you need to read it before signing.

## 6. Finalise the mortgage

Once your offer is accepted, the lender completes its checks on you and on the property. Read the mortgage offer carefully. Look for the rate, term, amortization, payment amount, penalty rules and prepayment privileges. If any number is unclear, ask for it to be explained before you accept.

## 7. Sign at the notary

The notary reviews the title, explains the documents, handles the signing and registers the transaction. Bring identification and the funds required. This is also a good moment to ask any last questions about the deed.

## 8. Plan for what comes next

Set a reminder for the end of your term, so you review your options in time instead of renewing by default. Keep a small reserve for the unexpected, because homes always have a surprise or two.

## A final thought

You do not have to understand every detail alone. Good guidance means someone translating the terms, comparing the options and letting you decide at your own pace.

## Common questions

**How early should I start?** Many people begin six to twelve months before they expect to buy, which leaves time to save, check their credit and learn how the costs fit together.

**Do I need a broker or can I go straight to my bank?** You can do either. A broker can compare several lenders for you, while a single institution can only offer its own products. Ask how each is paid.

**Does a pre-approval hurt my credit?** A lender may check your credit report, which can be recorded as an inquiry. One or two checks over a short period usually have a small effect, but ask before you proceed.

## Key takeaways

- Budget for the whole cost of ownership, not just the mortgage payment.
- Plan early for the welcome tax, notary fees and inspection.
- A pre-approval is a guide, not a commitment, and conditions in your offer protect you.
- Ask for every figure in writing before you sign at the notary.
