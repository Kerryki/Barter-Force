Insurance is not an exciting topic, and it is easy to put off. But the purpose of protection planning is simple: to make sure that a sudden event, such as an illness, an accident or a death, does not also become a financial disaster for the people who depend on you.

## Start with the question, not the product

Instead of asking "which insurance should I buy?", begin with "what would happen if..."

- If I could not work for six months, how would bills get paid?
- If I died, who would depend on my income, and for how long?
- If my home were damaged, could I afford to repair or replace it?
- If I were diagnosed with a serious illness, what extra costs would I face?

Your answers reveal the gaps. Only then does it make sense to talk about products.

## The main types of coverage

**Life insurance.** It pays a sum to your beneficiaries if you die. It is most relevant for people with dependants, a mortgage or debts that others would inherit. Two broad types exist: term insurance covers a set period and is generally less costly, while permanent insurance lasts a lifetime and costs more. Which is suitable depends on your needs, not on which is more impressive.

**Disability insurance.** It replaces part of your income if illness or injury prevents you from working. For many people, their ability to earn is their largest asset, so protecting it matters. Check the definition of disability, the waiting period and how long benefits last.

**Critical illness insurance.** It pays a lump sum if you are diagnosed with a serious condition that is listed in the policy. The list and the conditions vary, so read the definitions carefully.

**Home and tenant insurance.** Homeowners need coverage for the building and belongings, and for liability. Tenants usually need coverage for belongings and liability, and many landlords require it.

**Auto insurance.** It is required, and the details in Québec differ from other provinces because of the public plan for bodily injury. Understand what your policy covers for property damage and extras.

**Mortgage insurance.** Be careful with the name. Default insurance, required on some high-ratio mortgages, protects the lender. Life or disability insurance attached to a mortgage protects you or your family. They are different products.

## Group coverage at work

If your employer offers group benefits, find out exactly what they include, how much they cover and what happens if you leave. Group coverage is often a good base, but may not be enough alone and may end when employment does.

## How to avoid paying for too much

- **Measure the need.** Calculate what your dependants would need rather than relying on a round number.
- **Compare quotes.** Prices and terms differ between insurers.
- **Read the exclusions.** The fine print shows what the policy will not cover.
- **Ask how the advisor is paid.** Knowing how compensation works helps you evaluate the advice.
- **Do not overlap needlessly.** Review what you already have before adding more.

## Review as life changes

Protection needs are not permanent. A new child, a new home, a new business, a separation or a retirement can all change what you need. A review once a year, or after a major event, keeps your coverage aligned with your life.

## Questions to ask before you sign

1. What exactly is covered, and what is excluded?
2. How long does coverage last, and can the price change?
3. What is the waiting period before benefits begin?
4. What happens if I miss a payment?
5. What are the total costs over time?

## A short example

Consider a family with two young children, one main income and a mortgage. The first question is what would happen to the mortgage and daily costs if that income stopped, whether through death or long illness. That points toward life and disability coverage sized to the actual gap. Group benefits from work might cover part of it, so the plan would start by finding out how much, and for how long. Only the remaining gap would need to be filled. The same family might also find that home and auto coverage are fine, and that nothing more is needed there. A good review is as likely to remove an unnecessary policy as to add a new one.

## Common questions

**Is insurance worth it if I am young and healthy?** Needs depend on responsibilities rather than age. Someone without dependants or debts may need little, while a young parent with a mortgage may need a lot. Premiums are often lower when you are healthy, so it is worth understanding your options early.

**Can I change coverage later?** Often, but the terms, price and your eligibility can change as you age or if your health changes. Ask about renewal and conversion options.

## Key takeaways

- Begin with what could go wrong, then match coverage to those risks.
- Understand the type, the term, the exclusions and the total cost.
- Review your protection whenever your life changes.
- You should be told if you already have enough.
